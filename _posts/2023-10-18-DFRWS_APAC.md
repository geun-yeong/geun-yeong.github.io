I participated as a presenter at the **Digital Forensics Research Conference (DFRWS) 2023 APAC** held in Singapore.

This paper was especially meaningful to me because it was my first publication as a first author, and I was involved in every step of the research process, from identifying the research topic to conducting the experiments. I would like to sincerely thank my advisor, Professor Jungheum Park, for his careful guidance and attention to every detail, which made it possible for me to submit our work to such a prestigious conference.

**Chracer** identifies and extracts user information, such as visited URLs, from the memory of Chromium-based web browsers. Unlike conventional approaches that rely primarily on methods such as string searches, Chracer aims to accurately identify relevant artifacts without false positives by analyzing and locating the internal structures of classes and objects used to store URL information.

After finishing both a presentation and a research project, I often find myself thinking about what I could have done better. Nevertheless, I feel incredibly fortunate to have had the opportunity to meet so many talented and inspiring researchers through DFRWS. This experience has motivated me to continue pursuing high-quality research and to participate in even more outstanding conferences in the future.
